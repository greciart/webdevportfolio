## The problem

V7ad is a creative agency whose website was actively working against them. Everything sat too close together: no consistent spacing, no clear grid, headings and body copy competing at similar weights. The portfolio, the one thing that should have sold their work, was buried in the middle of that noise.

An agency selling design judgement cannot afford a site that demonstrates the absence of it. That was the real cost, more than any conversion metric.

## What I changed

**I imposed a grid before touching anything visual.** The original layout had no consistent column structure, which is why nothing lined up. Establishing that first solved most of the "it looks messy" complaint without changing a single colour.

**I rebuilt the spacing scale.** Fixed vertical rhythm between sections and consistent internal padding, so the eye gets a rest between ideas instead of scanning a continuous wall.

**I gave the portfolio its own architecture.** Work now sits in a dedicated grid with room around each piece, so a visitor can evaluate individual projects rather than seeing a collage.

**I flattened the type hierarchy to three levels.** Headline, section heading, body. Fewer distinct styles meant each one carries more meaning.

## Stack and build

Built on **WordPress** with **Elementor Pro**, using native containers rather than nested sections. That choice matters for performance: fewer wrapper elements means a shallower DOM, which keeps the page fast even with a heavy portfolio grid.

I kept third-party add-ons out of it deliberately. Every extra plugin is another script to load and another thing that breaks on update, and none of what this site needed justified that cost.

Responsiveness was handled per breakpoint rather than relying on automatic stacking, so the portfolio grid reflows into something deliberate on tablet and phone instead of collapsing into a single long column.

## Result

The agency ended up with a site that reads as considered rather than assembled, and one they can update themselves through WordPress without asking a developer every time they finish a project.

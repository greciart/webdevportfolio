Zeta Line is a Madrid company that designs, manufactures and installs office glass partitions and acoustic movable walls. Their clients are businesses that want to divide a floor without construction work, and hotels, schools or auditoriums that need to turn one large room into several smaller ones.

It's a technical product, quoted to measure, and nobody decides on it in one visit. Usually someone visits the site, compares options, forwards it to their architect and, if the site earned their trust, asks for a study. My job was to make that path work.

## The real problem

Zeta Line has six different systems: three partitions (Smart Glass, Twin Glass and Strong) and three movable walls (Maxparete, Domino and E-motion). For the people who make them, the differences are obvious. For an office manager who just wants to "close off the meeting room", they're six names that mean nothing.

And there was a second audience with the opposite needs: architects and interior designers. They look for specs, finishes and documentation to put the system into their project, without a sales pitch in the way.

If the site talks to both the same way, the client gets too much data and the architect gets too little.

## How I solved it

**Two lines before six products.** The site is organized around the company's two product lines, which match a question the client can answer: do you want a fixed division or one that moves? Partitions or movable walls. Each line has its own category page, and each system its own product page.

**Products explained by use.** Every page starts with what the system is for and what kind of space it fits. The technical details sit lower down, for whoever is looking for them.

**A separate path for architects.** A technical documentation section with specifications, construction details and installation manuals.

**Applications as a shortcut.** Offices, meeting rooms, hotels, schools, auditoriums, restaurants, events and training centers. Many people don't know which system they need, but they do know what kind of space they have.

**Questions answered before contact.** The FAQ covers what anyone asks before requesting a quote: the difference between the three partition types, acoustic insulation, finishes, whether the layout can change later, and where the movable walls get installed.

**One conversion.** "Request a study" on every page, with a direct call and WhatsApp as alternatives. Since everything is quoted to measure, the study is the first step of every sale, and it's the only thing the site asks for.

## Architecture

Home, two category pages and six product pages, plus completed projects, contact and the legal pages. Any product is two clicks from the home page.

The structure also does SEO work. Each product page targets a specific search ("office partitions", "acoustic movable walls" or the system's own name) and each category page groups them. Google knows what each URL answers, and someone coming from a search lands straight on the product they were looking for.

## Design

The client wanted something minimalist, clean and professional, with plenty of white space and an editorial approach. It fit the product, which sells with photography of finished spaces, glass and light.

I translated that into white, greys, a single line of the brand blue and light typography, with a clear hierarchy and generous spacing, so the photos of real projects get the attention.

## SEO and performance

On a site like this almost all the content is large photos, and it's easy to end up with a good-looking site that takes five seconds to load.

<Image src={zetalinePagespeed} alt="PageSpeed Insights report for zetaline.es on desktop: 91 performance, 91 accessibility, 96 best practices and 100 SEO." widths={[480, 1005]} sizes="(max-width: 768px) 100vw, 768px" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

On desktop, PageSpeed Insights gives it **91 performance, 91 accessibility, 96 best practices and 100 SEO.** What went into the project:

- Optimized images, with descriptive alt text on the project photos
- Page titles, meta descriptions, headings and URLs built around one keyword per page
- Site copy written with those searches in mind, without stuffing
- **Google Search Console** set up and the sitemap submitted
- A **Google Business Profile** created, so the company shows up in local searches in Madrid

That score is desktop. On mobile, a site this heavy on photography scores lower, and that's the next thing to optimize.

## Stack

- **WordPress** with **Elementor Pro**
- On-page SEO per product: one page, one target search
- Google Search Console and Google Business Profile
- Contact by form, phone and **WhatsApp**

## Result

A site that separates the client who needs guidance from the architect who needs data, explains six technical systems without overwhelming anyone and takes both to a study request, with a 91 performance score even though most of the content is photos.

## Selling a technical product?

If your site lists everything you make and clients still call to ask the basics, I'd look at the order first: what you tell first, to whom, and what you ask for at the end. That usually fixes more than a redesign.

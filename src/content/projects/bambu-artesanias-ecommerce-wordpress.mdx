## The situation

Bambú Artesanías makes handmade goods. Their products photograph beautifully and carry an obvious story, but they were selling through social media DMs, which meant every order was a manual conversation and nothing scaled.

The challenge with craft goods online is specific: the value lives in the making, and a generic template flattens that into just another product grid. If the store looks mass-produced, the products read as mass-produced too.

## Decisions that shaped the build

**I mapped the purchase flow before designing a single screen.** For an audience buying handmade items, the decision is slower and more emotional than buying a commodity. The flow needed room for that: discovery, then context about the maker, then the product, then checkout.

**Earth tones with real photography.** The palette came from the materials themselves. No stock imagery, because the whole proposition is that a person made this.

**Mobile ordering as the default case.** Most of their existing traffic came from Instagram, so the phone layout was designed first and the desktop version derived from it, not the other way round.

**A catalogue that respects browsing.** Craft shoppers scroll and compare. The grid is loose enough to let each piece register, with filtering that reflects how the maker actually categorises the work.

## The flow in practice

The architecture follows four stages:

1. **Home** establishes who makes the products and why they cost what they cost.
2. **Shop** presents the catalogue with categories drawn from the craft, not generic e-commerce labels.
3. **Product** carries the detail and the reviews that let someone commit.
4. **Checkout** collects only what is needed to fulfil the order.

After purchase there is a feedback prompt, which does double duty: it gives the client reviews for future buyers and tells them which pieces land.

## Stack

**WordPress** with **WooCommerce** for the store logic, built out in **Elementor Pro**. No purchased theme, so the layout follows the catalogue rather than the catalogue being squeezed into someone else's template.

Payment methods were set up for both card and local transfer, since a meaningful share of their customers do not use international cards.

## Outcome

The client moved from handling orders in DMs to a storefront that processes them on its own, and can add products without touching the design.
